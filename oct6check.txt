APRFree: full check, October 6, 2026
====================================

Everything that was checked after the October 6 feature round, what was found, what was fixed,
what was left alone and why, and what to do next. The earlier review is in checkup_oct5.txt.


1. RESULT IN SHORT
------------------
- 67 automated tests pass (63 before this check, 4 added). They also pass under the address,
  undefined-behaviour and thread sanitizers.
- The static analyzer reports nothing. Debug and Release builds have no warnings.
- No memory leaks, and memory stays flat when screens are opened and closed repeatedly.
- 21 problems found and fixed in this check: 10 in logic, 11 in the interface. None was a crash and
  none produced a wrong sum. The most important three:
    1. Undo could silently put back settings you had changed since (reminders off came back on).
    2. Reset Everything left a copy of your old data, and any exports, on the iPhone.
    3. On the smallest iPhone the main button read "Make a Pay..." at the normal text size.
- Two new kinds of test did the heavy lifting. One runs 1,320 random actions and checks the data
  after every one. The other checks the rewritten payoff engine against the original day-by-day
  method on 160 made-up situations. Both pass.
- Nothing has run on a real iPhone. Section 7 lists what was not verified.


2. HOW IT WAS CHECKED
---------------------

2.1 Automated
- Full suite on the iPhone 17 simulator (iOS 26.3): 67 of 67.
- Again with Address Sanitizer and Undefined Behavior Sanitizer: 67 pass, no reports.
- Again with Thread Sanitizer: 67 pass, no reports.
- Clean build with the static analyzer: no warnings.
- Release build: succeeds, no warnings.

2.2 Random use (new)
One test plays the app at random: 6 runs of 220 actions each, 1,320 in all. The actions are paying,
charging, editing a debt, undoing an entry, editing an entry's amount and day, statement check-ins,
paycheck splits with some lines left unpaid, extra-money splits, Undo, Redo, adding and deleting
debts, changing strategy and custom order, adding and removing incomes, switching to a fixed
amount, and moving the clock forward up to three weeks at a time.
After every action it checks that:
- no balance is negative, and every balance is a whole number of cents;
- the last entry for each debt left exactly the balance the debt has now;
- the balance history ends today, on today's totals, in date order, never below zero;
- a debt that owes money has no "paid off" date; installments paid never exceed installments;
- the undo history never holds more than its limit.
Every fourth action it also checks that:
- the data, saved and read back, is identical;
- a plan can be worked out, with interest that is a real, non-negative number;
- a $900 paycheck split never hands out more than is left after the buffer;
- each month's statement starts where the previous one ended, and the newest ends on today's total.
And after one action in five it takes the action back and puts it back, and checks the debts, the
entries and the settings are exactly where they were.
The numbers come from a fixed seed, so any failure can be run again exactly.

2.3 The engine against the original method (new)
The payoff engine was rewritten on October 6 to take each stretch between paydays in one step.
The original, which walked one day at a time, was put back as a test-only reference. 160 made-up
situations are run through both: random balances, rates and due days, promo rates ending partway,
part-paid minimums, pay-later plans on all three rhythms, weekly, two-weekly and monthly paydays,
all three strategies. Whether the plan finishes, the total interest (to the cent) and the payday
each debt is paid off must agree. They do, in all 160. More than half are plans that finish.

- In the source: still one class, no stored closures, no timers. One observer was added (for

3. WHAT WAS FOUND AND FIXED IN THIS CHECK
-----------------------------------------

3.1 Logic
 1. Undo put back things that are not part of the plan. Each undo step keeps a full copy of the
    data. Taking one back also took back anything else changed since: a reminder switch, the date
    of the last backup, a notice already read, even acceptance of the privacy policy. Undo and
    Redo now move the debts, the entries and the plan, and leave those alone.
 2. Four plan settings were not undo steps: the safety buffer (in two places), the fixed monthly
    amount, and the "minimum only on 0% promo cards" switch. Changing one and then undoing an
    earlier action silently reverted it. Each is now a step of its own. Typing a new buffer and
    then correcting it counts as one step, not two.
 3. Undo could bring back a payday that had since passed. If a step was taken before a payday and
    undone after it, the old payday came back. Paydays are now moved on after every Undo and Redo.
 4. Reset Everything did not erase everything. It left the copy of your data kept from before the
    upgrade, a file set aside as unreadable if there was one, and any CSV or PDF still waiting in
    the temporary folder. All are now removed, exports are kept in a folder of their own so they
    can be found to remove, and the widget's "hidden" switch is put back.
 5. Restoring a backup made before the privacy policy existed sent you back through the policy and
    the disclaimer. What has been accepted on this iPhone now stays accepted across a restore.
 6. The widget would show wrong dates on an iPhone set to the Buddhist or Japanese calendar. It
    turned a calendar day into a date using the iPhone's own calendar, where the year 2026 means
    something else. It now uses the Gregorian calendar, which is what the days are.
 7. A debt's amounts were not always held to the cent. Found by the random test: a minimum of
    17.869999999999997 in memory was saved and read back as 17.87, so the data was not identical
    after a restart. Nothing typed in the app produces such a number, but nothing prevented one
    either. Adding and editing a debt now round every money field to the cent.
 8. The statement check-in waited for loans. "Check In 7 Balances" counted two loans, whose
    interest the app already works out itself, payment by payment. It now waits for cards only.
    Loans can still be checked in. Paid-off loans are no longer listed; a card at $0.00 still is,
    because it can have a new balance.
 9. Extra Money with no income set said "your next payday stays" followed by today's date. It now
    leaves that out. A split with nothing in it now says why, instead of showing an empty list.
10. With the app left open on screen past midnight, it stayed on yesterday's plan until closed and
    reopened. It now notices midnight.

3.2 Interface
11. The top of the check-in sheet was cut off at the left edge of the screen.
12. Check-in fields were filled with the current figures, so typing a new balance added digits to
    the old one. They now start empty and show the current figure in grey: the first key typed
    starts the new number, and an empty field means "the statement agrees".
13. The open statement had a fixed height, so larger text was cut off, and its Check In button sat
    inside another button, which can make a tap do the wrong thing. It now grows with its text and
    the buttons are side by side.
14. Amounts broke in the middle of a number at large text sizes ("$42,274.7" over "3"), on the
    statement and in the income list. Amounts now stay on one line and shrink slightly if they must.
15. On the iPhone SE the main button read "Make a Pay..." at the default text size, the bar being
    a few points too narrow for that screen. The name now shrinks a little instead of being cut.
16. At large text sizes the same button was cut on every iPhone. The top bar is one fixed row, so
    its text now stops growing at the size where it still fits.
17. At large text sizes, balances on the card strips ran into the "BALANCE" label above them. The
    cards are drawn at a fixed size, so their text now stops growing at the size where it still fits.
18. At the largest text sizes the income row squeezed the name to "Pay-ch...". It now stacks the
    name, the rhythm and the amount.
19. The income editor's list of next paydays said each date twice ("Fri, Oct 9 ... Oct 9, 2026").
    It now says "Fri, Oct 9 ... In 3 days".
20. Deleting a debt did not say it could be undone. The confirmation now does.
21. The check-in button said "Balances" where it now means cards: "Check In 5 Cards".

3.3 Found and fixed earlier on October 6, while building the features
Listed here so the day's record is in one place.
- Undoing an older entry on data from before October 4 applied every later payment a second
  time. All of your own entries were of that kind. Rewritten, with a test.
- The widget's eye button only changed the widget that was tapped. It now updates all of them.
- In the PDF report, two column headings overlapped and one chart had odd tick values (58%, 29%).


4. CHECKED AND FOUND CORRECT
----------------------------
Things looked at specifically, with nothing to fix.
- The fast engine gives the same interest and payoff paydays as the day-by-day one (2.3).
- No order of the seven debts that take extra payments costs less interest than Avalanche: all
  5,040 orders, each run as a whole plan. They take about 4 seconds in the unoptimised test build.
- Pay-later plans: one payment settles one installment, anything beyond it comes off the end of the
  plan, and there is no nine-day wait. This matches Klarna's and Affirm's published terms.
- Paydays: weekly and two-weekly keep their weekday; monthly keeps its day and handles the 29th
  to 31st; two pay days that fall on the same date in a short month count once; several incomes
  on one day are one payday with both amounts; a passed payday moves on by its own rhythm.
- A whole paycheck split marked paid uses up that payday for the incomes behind it and no others.
  Leaving one line unpaid leaves the payday where it is. Extra money never uses one up.
- Undo then Redo returns the debts, entries and settings to exactly where they were (2.2).
- Editing an entry's amount re-works what it counted as. A $35.00 minimum cut to $20.00 becomes a
  partial payment, and the next payment then finishes the minimum instead of being all extra.
- Moving an entry to another day changes the past: the balance history on the days between follows.
- The one-time due-date repair only moves dates back, never forward; it leaves a debt with no
  payments alone, and a debt added after its due day; and it does not run twice.
- Arithmetic read off the screen: the statement (start, minus paid, plus charges, plus interest,
  equals today) adds up to the cent before and after a check-in; the paycheck split totals the
  paycheck less the buffer; card totals fall by exactly the card lines paid.
- Files saved before October 6 open, with every date on the same calendar day, including a file
  written in a time zone where midnight is a different day here.


5. LEFT AS IT IS: THINGS TO KNOW OR DECIDE
------------------------------------------
None of these is a crash or a wrong sum.

Two screens give different "interest" for the same month.
- Insights, "September recap", says "Interest, about $324.69". The September statement says
  "Interest recorded $169.47". Both are right by their own definition: the statement counts
  interest actually recorded (the interest part of loan payments, and statement check-ins), while
  Insights also estimates what the cards charged. Until check-ins are used every month the two
  will differ, and someone will notice. Once they are, Insights should show the recorded figure.

Decisions still open (also in preparation.txt and FUTURE_DEVELOPMENT.txt).
- The debt-free month is the month of the last due date covered, which can be the month after the
  last payment is made.
- A minimum marked paid inside the nine-day wait is recorded as an extra payment.
- One safety buffer is taken from every paycheck, whichever income it is.
- A fixed monthly amount is paid on one day of the month.
- Statement titles are in Snell Roundhand because iOS has no Apple Chancery.

Limits of what the app knows.
- Card balances still only move when something is recorded or checked in.
- Credit utilization over time is measured against today's limits; past limits are not kept.
- A debt deleted before October 6 has no record of when it was deleted. The history treats it as
  gone from the day after its last entry.
- Only payments and charges can be edited. Balance changes and check-ins can be undone and redone.
- Undo keeps the last 15 actions. Each is a full copy of the data, kept in a second file.

Text size.
- The fixes in 3.2 cover the worst cases. Much of the app still uses fixed text sizes (card faces,
  tile figures, the number pad), so larger text settings enlarge some things and not others.
  Nothing overlaps or is cut at the size tested; the largest two sizes were not tested.

Forward compatibility.
- A file saved by a newer version of the app, opened by this one, is read as far as this one
  understands it. Anything new in it would be lost on the next save. This can only happen by
  restoring a newer backup into an older app, but there is no guard against it yet.

8. SUGGESTIONS, MOST IMPORTANT FIRST
------------------------------------
 1. Commit this. The repository holds one commit from before October 6; everything since is
    uncommitted. Commit the feature round and this check separately so either can be backed out.
 2. Open the app on your own data, accept the two documents, and look at one thing in particular:
    the "Due dates corrected" notice for Chase. If November 9 is not right, change it under Edit.
 3. Run it on your iPhone through a full pay cycle and a month end. Most of section 7 clears itself
    that way, and the monthly check-in can only really be judged with a real statement in hand.
 4. Make Insights use recorded interest once a month has check-ins, so the two screens agree (5).
 5. Add a monthly reminder for the statement check-in. The button is there; nothing prompts it.
 6. Guard against opening data from a newer version: refuse the backup with a clear message, and
    for the app's own file, open it read-only and say "update APRFree" rather than saving over it.
 7. Give Dynamic Type one proper pass: decide which text scales and which is art, and test the two
    largest sizes. The fixes here were for what broke, not a design.
 8. Add UI tests for the five flows done by hand in 2.6. They were checked today and will need
    checking again after every change; a test would do it in a minute.
 9. Keep the random test and the day-by-day reference. When the engine changes, change the
    reference only if the rules changed, never to make a difference go away.
10. Let balance changes and check-ins be edited, not only undone.
11. Record a credit limit's history, so utilization in the past is right after a limit changes.
12. Before the App Store: everything in preparation.txt, and a lawyer's read of the two documents.
13. Decide the design canvas: update it in a session of its own or retire it. It is out of date
    (FUTURE_DEVELOPMENT.txt, section 9).
