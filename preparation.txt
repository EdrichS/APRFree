2. BANK NAMES, TYPEFACES AND TRADEMARKS (READ THIS ONE CAREFULLY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
What the app does now: no logo artwork anywhere. Each bank or provider name is drawn as text, on a
card in that brand's colours, in a typeface chosen to suggest its wordmark.

What it does not do, and cannot: use the banks' real logo typefaces. Those wordmarks are custom
lettering or proprietary fonts. None of them ships with iOS and none can be bundled into an app
without a licence from its owner. Every typeface in APRFree is one that already ships with iOS:

      Chase              Avenir Next Bold, capitals
      Capital One        Avenir Next Bold Italic
      Bank of America    Avenir Next Condensed Demi Bold, capitals
      Citi               DIN Alternate Bold, lower case
      American Express   Helvetica Neue Condensed Bold, capitals
      Klarna             Avenir Next Heavy
      Affirm             Avenir Medium, lower case

The risk: these names are trademarks. Naming a bank so a user can label their own debt is ordinary
descriptive use. Dressing the name up to look like the bank's logo, on a card in its colours, moves
closer to imitating the brand, and that is what App Review guideline 5.2.1 (using third-party
trademarks without permission) and each bank's own brand rules are about. Text-in-a-similar-font is
lower risk than a real logo, but it is not zero risk. The card product names in the add flow
(Freedom Unlimited, Sapphire, Quicksilver, Venture, Double Cash, Gold Card and so on) are trademarks
too.

What lowers the risk:
[x] No logo images, and a note in Settings saying the app is not affiliated with or endorsed by any
    bank or lender named in it.
[x] One switch turns all of the styling off: in APRFree/Design/Theme.swift set
    Wordmark.styled to false and every name is drawn in the plain system font, as written. If App
    Review or a bank objects, that is a one-line change and a resubmit.
[ ] Keep bank names and bank-styled cards OUT of the App Store listing: not in the app name, the
    subtitle, the keywords, the icon, or the screenshots. Guideline 2.3.7 is about trademarked terms
    in metadata, and screenshots are where a brand owner is most likely to notice.
[ ] Say "not affiliated with any bank or lender" in the App Store description as well.
[?] Decide how far to go before launch. The safest version ships with Wordmark.styled = false and
    neutral card colours. The current version looks better and carries more risk. A middle path is
    styled names in the app, neutral demo cards in every screenshot.
[?] If you want certainty, read each brand's published trademark guidelines or ask a lawyer.


3. PRIVACY
----------
The facts, checked against the source on October 5: there is no network code in the app, no
analytics, no ads, no account, and no third-party SDK. Data lives in one file in the app's own
storage, written with iOS complete file protection (unreadable while the iPhone is locked).

[x] Privacy manifests (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) added for the app and the widget. They declare the
    one "required reason" API the app uses, UserDefaults, with reasons CA92.1 (the app's own
    settings) and 1C8F.1 (the app group shared with the widget). They are in the built bundles.
[ ] App Store Connect, App Privacy section: answer "Data Not Collected". That is true only while
    the facts above stay true.
[ ] Write and host a privacy policy. A policy URL is required for every app, including ones that
    collect nothing. It should say, in plain words: everything stays on the device; nothing is sent
    to the developer; backups and exports are files the user chooses where to save; reminders are
    local notifications; the data is included in the user's own iPhone backup (iCloud or computer)
    if they have backups turned on; how to delete everything (Settings, Reset Everything, or delete
    the app).
[ ] If you ever add iCloud sync, crash reporting, analytics or any SDK: update the privacy answers,
    the policy and both privacy manifests in the same release.
[x] The widget marks balances as privacy sensitive, so iOS can hide them while the iPhone is locked.
[?] There is no Face ID or passcode lock on the app itself. Not required by Apple, but people expect
    it from an app that shows what they owe. It is on the future list.


4. WHAT APP REVIEW WILL LOOK AT FOR A FINANCE APP
-------------------------------------------------
[ ] Guideline 3.2.1 (viii) says apps for financial trading, investing or money management should be
    submitted by the financial institution that performs the service. APRFree performs no financial
    service: it never connects to a bank, never moves money and never offers credit. Say exactly
    that in the review notes so it is read as a personal calculator and tracker, which is what it is.
[x] In-app disclaimer (Settings): a planning tool, not financial advice; dates and interest are
    estimates from the numbers entered.
[ ] Put the same disclaimer in the App Store description.
[ ] Keep claims in the listing honest (guideline 2.3). Avoid "guaranteed", "get out of debt in X
    months", or a promised amount saved. "See an estimated debt-free date" is accurate.
[?] The name "APRFree" could be read as an offer of 0% credit. If review raises it, the subtitle
    should make clear it is a payoff planner (for example "Debt payoff planner").
[ ] Review notes to include with the submission: no account or sign-in; works fully offline; how to
    reach the main screens (finish setup, add one card with any numbers, tap Make a Payment); the
    notification permission is only for local due-date and payday reminders.
[ ] Age rating questionnaire: nothing in the app should raise it above the lowest rating.
[x] Category is set to Finance in the project.
[x] Export compliance: ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption is set to NO in the app's Info.plist (the app
    uses no encryption of its own, only iOS file protection), so uploads will not stop to ask.
[?] Where to sell it. The app is English only, US dollars only and lists US banks. Starting in the
    US alone is the simple choice. Selling in the EU requires declaring your trader status in App
    Store Connect.
[ ] Price: if it is free with no purchases, no banking or tax forms are needed.


5. THINGS THE APP STILL NEEDS BEFORE A PUBLIC RELEASE
-----------------------------------------------------
Blocking:
[ ] App icon: 1024 x 1024, no transparency. iOS 26 also takes a layered icon made in Icon Composer
    with light, dark and tinted looks; a single flat icon is still accepted. No bank logos or card
    art in it.
[ ] Run on a real iPhone and go through every screen.
[ ] Confirm reminders actually fire (due date: 9:00 the day before; payday: 9:00 that day).
[ ] Confirm the widget on a device, all four sizes including the two Lock Screen ones.
[ ] Exercise CSV export, PDF summary, Back Up Now and Restore end to end, including restoring a
    backup onto a second device.

Strongly recommended, because strangers will rely on the numbers:
[?] Card balances do not grow on their own. Interest only shows up when the user edits a balance or
    adds a charge. For one person who knows this, fine. For the public, the balance will drift from
    the real statement and the payoff date with it. A monthly "enter your statement balance" prompt
    (already on the future list) is the fix.
[?] Decide the open questions in section 9.

Compatibility:
[?] The minimum is iOS 26.0, so only iPhones already on iOS 26 can install it. Lowering it means
    guarding the Liquid Glass effects and retesting. Decide before launch; raising it later is easy,
    lowering it is work.
[ ] iPhone only, portrait only. Reviewers sometimes test iPhone apps on an iPad, where they run in
    a small iPhone-sized window. Launch it once in an iPad simulator to be sure nothing breaks.
[ ] Test the smallest iPhone that runs iOS 26 (iPhone SE size) and the largest.
[ ] Test with the largest text sizes, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, Increase Contrast and Dark Mode.
    The code has labels and Reduce Motion handling, but only a real pass will find the gaps.
[ ] Test with the iPhone's Region set outside the US. Money is always shown as US dollars, but the
    number pad's decimal mark follows the region (one bug from this was fixed on October 5).
[ ] Test changing time zone. Saved dates now move with the time zone so a due date stays on the
    same day; verify it on a device by changing the zone in Settings.

7. BUILDING, TESTING AND UPLOADING
----------------------------------
[ ] Version numbers: MARKETING_VERSION is 1.0 and CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION is 1 on every target.

    for every upload.
[ ] Archive with the Release configuration (Product, Archive) and upload from the Organizer.
[x] Debug-only launch arguments (-onboarding, -seedDemo, -screen) are compiled out of Release

[ ] TestFlight first. Internal testers need no review. External testers need a short beta review.

[ ] There is no crash reporting in the app (by design: nothing leaves the device). Crashes from
    users who share diagnostics show up in Xcode's Organizer and in TestFlight.
[ ] Apple raises the minimum Xcode and SDK for uploads every spring. The project is on Xcode 26.3.


8. PROTECTING PEOPLE'S DATA ACROSS UPDATES
------------------------------------------
Once real people use it, their only copy of their data is the file on their iPhone.
[x] A saved file that cannot be understood is no longer overwritten: it is set aside as
    data-unreadable.json next to the data file, and the app starts fresh.
[x] If iOS starts the app while the iPhone is still locked and the file cannot be read yet, the app
    waits and loads it when it comes to the front, and saves nothing over it in the meantime.
[x] Numbers read from a backup are kept inside sane ranges.
[ ] Before changing any saved model (Debt, PaymentRecord, PlanSettings, AppData): add the new field
    as optional or with a default, bump AppData.version, and add a test that loads a file written
    by the previous release. Keep a copy of a real saved file from every released version for this.
[ ] Never remove or rename a saved field, or an enum case, without a decoder that still accepts the
    old value (PayFrequency shows how: "twice a month" still loads).
[ ] Mention Back Up Now in the release notes of any update that changes saved data.


9. OPEN DECISIONS THAT AFFECT WHAT USERS SEE
--------------------------------------------
[?] "Highest Balance" ranks by monthly interest in dollars (balance x APR / 12), not by raw
    balance. The name suggests raw balance. Either rename it or change the rule.
[?] Pay-later installments have no 9-day lock: paying the next one early advances the plan. Cards
    and loans do have the lock. Confirm that is what you want.
[?] The debt-free month is the month of the due date the final payment covers, which can be the
    month after the last payment is actually made (pay the last dollar on Nov 5 toward a Dec 1 due
    date and it says December). Some people will expect November.
[?] Make a Payment always belongs to the next unsplit payday and, when every line is marked paid,
    moves the payday on. Splitting a bonus the same way uses up the following payday. Consider a
    separate "extra money, not a paycheck" path.
[?] In a split, a minimum whose due date is still inside the 9-day lock is recorded as an extra
    payment if it is marked paid early, and the due date stays. The split line says "pay from" the
    date it will count. Decide whether marking it early should be blocked, or allowed and counted.


10. SETUP ANIMATIONS (FOR WHEN YOU TAKE IT TO CLAUDE DESIGN)
------------------------------------------------------------
Every animation and transition in first-run setup lives in one file:
APRFree/Screens/OnboardingMotion.swift. It has eight named slots (step, hero, headline, selection,
list, progress, press, finish), each with a plain default and a Reduce Motion fallback. The setup
screens never write an animation themselves; they ask for a slot by name. To change how setup
moves, point at a slot in that file and say what it should do instead.
